What is a Broker Opinion of Value, and how is it different from an appraisal?

A Broker Opinion of Value is a broker’s written estimate of the price a property is likely to sell for. Owners use it to decide whether to sell, hold or refinance, and to set a list price. It is not an appraisal and is not prepared under USPAP. A lender will usually require an appraisal from a licensed appraiser for a loan.

How do you value an apartment building?

We start with the rent roll and the trailing 12 months of income and expenses. We adjust taxes, insurance and management to what the most likely buyer will carry, compare rents to nearby properties, and apply the cap rate buyers are paying for that age, size and location. Recent sales of similar buildings are the cross-check. You see every step in writing.

What should I send to get a valuation?

A current rent roll and a trailing 12-month operating statement are enough to start. The most recent property tax bill, the insurance premium and a list of recent capital work make the number tighter. If you do not have clean statements, send what you have.

Will my property taxes change after a sale in Indiana?

They can. Indiana assesses property every year as of January 1, and a recent sale price is evidence an assessor can use. Buyers price that risk in, so we underwrite taxes at a reassessed level when we value your property. Indiana caps property tax at 2% of gross assessed value for residential rental property and 3% for other commercial property, before voter-approved referendums. This is general information, not tax advice.

Do you represent both the buyer and the seller?

We represent one side of a deal. If a buyer and a seller in the same transaction both want Parlato Real Estate, Indiana law requires written consent from both before a broker can act for both.
